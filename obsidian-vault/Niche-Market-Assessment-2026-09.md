# Vendora: keep, change or add niches?

**Decision research for Aviy · United Kingdom · 30 September 2026**

## Recommendation

Keep convenience, off-licence and international grocery as the initial recruitment pool. Narrow the customer much further: an owner-operated shop with one to three locations, several suppliers, an existing till, and a recurring delivery/invoice/credit problem that its current tools do not resolve conveniently.

Keep pet shops, greengrocers and health-food shops as comparison candidates. Merge newsagent into convenience for launch marketing. Defer specialist development for vape/CBD, butchery/fishmongery and bakery/deli. Turn market trading into an operating-mode tag. Narrow discount/pound to clearance and mixed-lot retail if interviews reveal a viable repeat task.

Do not add a new production niche now. Beauty/hair-supply, hardware/DIY and small farm shops are sensible research alternatives, but this research does not establish that any is an easier market or a stronger opportunity than the current candidates.

This is a conditional product strategy, not demonstrated product-market fit. The biggest change from the earlier advice is that invoice matching, discrepancy detection and supplier-credit follow-up already have direct competitors. Vendora cannot claim that this category is unserved.

## Method and limits

The assessment combines the existing Vendora shop registry and app scope with current UK trade-body publications, official product pages, supplier documentation and government guidance. It distinguishes sector demand from willingness to pay for an additional app. Published vendor capabilities are evidence of competitive positioning, not independently tested functionality or customer satisfaction.

The review used accessible pages and search-indexed extracts where complete page retrieval was unavailable. No supplier demos were attended, no paid research was purchased, no owners were interviewed and no integrations were tested for this assessment. The report therefore proposes field tests rather than invented demand percentages, revenue forecasts or numerical niche scores.

Store categories overlap: an international grocer may also be a convenience store and off-licence. These are not 12 mutually exclusive addressable markets. Organic-food spending is not the size of independent health-food retail; pet population is not the number of potential SaaS buyers; chain-store closures do not establish the economics of independent discount shops. Comparable, recent public counts and software-spend data were not established for every niche.

## What the evidence changes

### There is demand for efficiency, but budgets are constrained

ACS forecasts convenience-sector sales of £49.1bn in 2026, growth of 0.6%, against average inflation of 2.9% over the preceding year. Investment is around £900m annually in 2025 and 2026. These figures describe financial pressure; they do not prove that retailers will buy Vendora. [S1]

Bira's early-2026 member survey also reports weak investment confidence. Its results cover a member sample across several sectors and should not be treated as a census of all independent shops. [S2]

**Implication:** demonstrate a specific saving and fast first use. A large feature menu, a dashboard or a low subscription price is insufficient evidence of value.

### The competition reaches beyond tills

| Competitive layer | Verified public offering | Consequence for Vendora |
|---|---|---|
| General retail POS | Square offers a free tier with basic inventory and paid advanced tools/purchasing. [S3] | A generic stock app faces a strong free/basic alternative. |
| Convenience-specialist POS | ShopMate advertises wholesaler price files, electronic delivery notes and handheld receiving/stock functions. [S4] | Investigate the owner's actual installed system before proposing duplicate work. |
| Connected invoice operations | sruu advertises supplier-invoice capture, delivery matching, discrepancy detection and accounts posting. [S5] | Invoice checking is an existing software category. |
| Hospitality purchasing | growyze advertises invoice scanning, price changes and requested-credit tracking. [S6] | Credit follow-up is not a unique feature, even if Vendora targets different users. |
| Integrated retail operations | Stockagile describes receiving against purchase orders with discrepancy reasons. [S7] | A product receiving screen alone is not differentiation. |
| Supplier-provided apps | Bestway supports mobile browsing, scanning and ordering; SNapp supports news deliveries, claims and credits. [S8, S9] | An independent layer must add cross-supplier or unfinished-work value. |

No conclusion here relies on competitors' advertised savings percentages. Their current total costs, minimum contracts and integration coverage need like-for-like quotations before claiming that Vendora is cheaper or simpler.

## The 12 niche decisions

### 1. Convenience / mini-mart — KEEP as a launch candidate

**Evidence:** ACS documents ongoing cost pressure. Square and ShopMate already cover substantial retail operations, and sruu addresses invoice reconciliation. [S1, S3–S5]

**Opening to test:** incidents that fall between receiving, the owner and accounts: a damaged delivery is photographed but not claimed; a disputed line is never resolved; a staff member notices a price change but no one decides what to do.

**Best buyer:** the owner of a small store or group who personally checks supplier bills and is accountable for the result. Staff may capture evidence, but are not necessarily the purchasing decision-maker.

**Acquisition:** direct local visits and owner referrals first. Wholesale and trade-body channels are later possibilities, not partnerships already obtained.

**Main adoption risk:** entering the same delivery into two systems. A well-configured existing EPOS may be sufficient.

**Interview test:** follow the last discrepancy from delivery to final credit or rejection. Ask the owner to demonstrate the current tool. Continue only if an unresolved, recurring gap remains after that demonstration.

### 2. Off-licence — KEEP; practical first pilot for Aviy

**Evidence:** WSTA's public July-2026 market summary describes declining alcohol sales across on- and off-trade. That is market pressure rather than a growth thesis. Existing convenience systems already handle supplier pricing and deliveries. [S10, S4]

**Opening to test:** the handling of cases versus individual units, damaged items, supplier-cost changes and follow-up on disputed charges. The app should work from actual invoice data, with confirmed pack quantities, instead of assuming a standard case size.

**Why first:** Aviy's retail experience and access can reduce the cost of recruiting and understanding early users. That is a founder advantage, not evidence that every off-licence is underserved.

**Boundary:** lead with purchasing administration and measurable outcomes. Avoid expanding the first release into tax calculations, licence management or a complete compliance product.

**Interview test:** ask for a recent invoice correction or unresolved supplier claim, the time spent and the final outcome. Reject the assumption that high stock value automatically means high willingness to pay.

### 3. International grocer — KEEP, but qualify by operations

**Evidence:** XEPOS explicitly targets Asian supermarkets with multilingual and fresh-stock capabilities; Ethco markets online-shop services to ethnic grocers. These are signs of existing targeting, not an empty market. [S11, S12]

**Opening to test:** fragmented purchasing across suppliers, inconsistent item descriptions, case/unit conversion and mixed fresh/packaged deliveries. These problems must be observed rather than inferred from an owner's background.

**Best buyer:** a shop with several actual supplier formats and material manual reconciliation. A supermarket with sophisticated integration may be a poor prospect.

**Acquisition:** nearby business clusters and referrals can be efficient if Aviy can demonstrate the workflow in person.

**Main risk:** catalogue normalisation can consume more labour than the app saves. Translation alone is not a defensible product.

**Interview test:** compare two recent supplier invoices and show how the owner identifies equivalent products, checks quantities and resolves an error. Public evidence for this segment's extra software spending remains weak.

### 4. Pet shop — KEEP as the strongest comparison candidate

**Evidence:** UK Pet Food's research demonstrates a large pet-owning population, but does not quantify the addressable independent-shop software market. Epos Now and Lightspeed explicitly offer pet retailers inventory and purchasing tools. [S13–S15]

**Opening to test:** special-order and repeat-customer fulfilment: request, exact variant, supplier order, arrival, reservation and collection. The question is whether requests are lost between existing systems.

**Buyer and acquisition:** owner-managed pet retailers, reached through local visits and referrals. Bestpets is a relevant supplier ecosystem, not an agreed distribution partner.

**Main risk:** the installed till already handles the proposed process. Inventory-based reservations also need a reliable stock source; the separate app cannot promise live availability from incomplete movements.

**Interview test:** follow the last five special requests. Identify missed sales, uncollected products or staff time, then test the actual proposed improvement. Keep veterinary advice and medicine-specific functions outside this initial scope.

### 5. Greengrocer — KEEP as a narrow pilot, not a full launch bundle

**Evidence:** competing greengrocery systems advertise scale integration, weighted pricing and dated-stock handling. [S16]

**Opening to test:** receiving quality and usable yield: bought weight, rejected weight, usable cost/kg and the supporting evidence for a supplier claim. This can be useful without recording every sale.

**Buyer:** the owner or buyer who makes receiving and replenishment decisions daily.

**Main risk:** extensive logging in a fast physical workflow. Automated markdown and ordering recommendations require dependable stock and sales information; the current standalone model must not imply those inputs exist automatically.

**Interview test:** observe a real delivery and time the extra work. Use two or three product lines first. Continue if the owner actually uses the result to claim, adjust purchasing or price a product.

**Evidence confidence:** competitor presence is clear; the size and payment appetite of the specific proposed customer group are not established.

### 6. Health-food shop — KEEP for later comparison

**Evidence:** the organic market has continued to grow overall, but trade coverage of the 2026 Soil Association report says independent organic retail declined 1.1% in 2025. Overall organic growth should not be presented as proof that independent health-food shops are expanding. [S17, S18]

**Opening to test:** cash tied up in slow or expiring lines, and customer requests that are forgotten between ordering and arrival.

**Best buyer:** a retailer with demonstrable write-offs or a substantial special-order process, rather than a broadly defined interest in wellness.

**Main risk:** batch-level data entry and competition from the current retail system. Supplements, refill groceries and organic food are materially different assortments.

**Interview test:** inspect a recent write-off and the shop's current date-checking method. Record actual cost, frequency and whether the action could have been taken in time. Avoid medical recommendations or health-information collection.

### 7. Newsagent / CTN — MERGE into convenience for launch marketing

**Evidence:** Smiths News describes a long-term revenue decline of roughly 3–5% a year in its core business. SNapp already provides claim and credit functions, including zero-credit claims. This does not mean all mixed newsagents are failing. [S19, S9]

**Opening to test:** unresolved cases across suppliers or general shop administration beyond the news wholesaler's system.

**Main risk:** duplicating a free supplier tool without being able to submit or reconcile information directly. Complaints about an existing app do not establish that a separate app would remove the same underlying problem.

**Interview test:** watch the owner use SNapp for the last unsuccessful claim. Identify whether the blocker is the interface, supplier policy, evidence, delayed collection or a missing credit. Vendora cannot solve all five in the same way.

Keep the shop-type label for discovery; do not give newspaper functionality its own initial product roadmap.

### 8. Vape / CBD — SPLIT the concept and DEFER dedicated development

**Evidence:** HMRC's Vaping Products Duty and duty-stamp transition begins on 1 October 2026. CBD foods have a separate novel-food framework under FSA guidance. Lightspeed already targets vape retail. [S20–S22]

**Opening to test:** product/supplier documentation, faulty-product returns and the impact of actual cost changes on buying decisions.

**Main risk:** maintaining product-specific rules while competing with established retail tools. Vaping liquids, devices and CBD foods should not inherit one assumed compliance workflow.

**Interview test:** determine the costliest repeat operational task after asking about recent changes. Do not presume a one-time regulatory transition supports a durable subscription.

Existing generic inventory or receiving functions could still serve a vape retailer. Deferring a specialist bundle does not require deleting customer records or refusing ordinary retail use. A future specialist offering needs domain expertise and a clear maintenance owner.

### 9. Butcher / fishmonger — DEFER specialist bundle; distinguish requirements

**Evidence:** myEPOS and Cunninghams describe butcher-specific scale and order capabilities. National Craft Butchers publishes sector surveys. Seafish explicitly notes that its retail datasets do not cover seafood bought through independent fishmongers, limiting confident market-size claims. [S23–S26]

**Opening to test:** customer preparation orders or delivery-quality evidence, initially avoiding a complete production/traceability system.

**Main risk:** transforming whole stock into cuts, actual weight, yield and waste is more complex than selling packaged units. Butchers and fishmongers share some needs but are not interchangeable workflows.

**Interview test:** follow one preparation order and ask where instructions or quantities get lost. If value depends on scale integration and production accounting from day one, this is a materially larger product investment.

Keep exploratory access; do not advertise full readiness based on a generic stock screen or a sample yield calculator.

### 10. Bakery / deli — DEFER production; allow resale-oriented deli discovery

**Evidence:** Cybake already covers bakery production, recipes, ordering, waste and forecasting; Cunninghams advertises integration with it. [S27, S28]

**Opening to test:** custom-order handoff or a limited production decision whose inputs can be collected reliably.

**Main risk:** a bakery makes stock from ingredients, while a resale deli may mainly receive and resell it. Forecasting bake quantities needs more than inventory balances: relevant demand history and operational context matter.

**Interview test:** establish whether the store manufactures, finishes products or merely resells. Ask what happened with the last overproduction event or missed custom order.

A small deli whose main issue is supplier discrepancies may fit the same launch workflow. A production bakery needing recipe costing is a separate expansion decision.

### 11. Market trader — RECLASSIFY as an operating mode

**Evidence:** NMTF is investigating connectivity problems affecting payments and other trading tasks. SumUp provides a free POS plan, with payment transaction fees; basic mobile selling is therefore already served. [S29, S30]

**Opening to test:** comparing events using takings, goods sold, pitch costs, travel, labour time and returns. Label contribution carefully; it is not automatically full business profit.

**Main risk:** diverse products, irregular trading and limited evidence for willingness to pay for another app. A produce stall and a craft stall are not one niche merely because both trade outdoors.

**Interview test:** compare the owner's last two events and observe how they decided which to repeat. Recruit recurring traders with meaningful inventory before assuming occasional sellers are a sustainable subscription audience.

Use market trading as a channel tag alongside the product type. Offline resilience is relevant, but not by itself a reason to buy.

### 12. Discount / pound shop — REFRAME as clearance and mixed-lot retail

**Evidence:** mainstream retail systems already provide basic stock tools. Bira documents pressure across independents, but public evidence found here does not establish the economics of independent clearance shops specifically. Do not generalise from Poundland's corporate restructuring. [S2, S3]

**Opening to test:** lot purchase cost, usable units, damaged items, markdowns and money recovered from each lot.

**Main risk:** inconsistent products and uncertain sell-through. A one-off break-even calculator is useful but may not support repeat paid usage. Tracking recovery requires actual sales or sufficiently reliable periodic counts.

**Interview test:** take one recently completed lot and reconstruct its outcome. Ask what the buyer would change on the next purchase and whether data collection can be sustained.

Keep this hypothesis available; do not use 'pound shop' as a broad feature bundle.

## Should another niche replace these?

| Alternative | Why worth investigating | Existing competitive evidence / limitation | Decision now |
|---|---|---|---|
| Beauty and hair-supply retailers | Variants, customer requests, slow stock and multiple suppliers | Lightspeed specifically supports beauty variants and supplier reporting. [S31] No comparative willingness-to-pay evidence was established. | Research comparator only; not a replacement yet. |
| Hardware / DIY independents | Special orders, trade customers and supplier packs | ETABS advertises tool/hardware supplier catalogues and ordering. [S32] More product and trade-account complexity. | Later discovery if accessible owners show a stronger repeated task. |
| Small farm shops | Receiving from local suppliers and fresh-stock handling overlap | RST and CSY already address farm-shop retail and weighing. [S33] Frequently cited £1.4bn sector research is from 2022, not a fresh 2026 market estimate. [S34] | Natural later adjacency, not an escape from competition. |
| Garden centres | Seasonal purchasing and plant losses | HTA reports a stronger 2025, but weather-sensitive 2026 trading; Epos Now offers a garden-centre system. [S35, S36] | Do not add to the current release. |
| Cafés / hospitality | Purchasing and supplier-credit problems are closely related | growyze directly addresses that workflow. [S6] Recipes and production add scope. | No evidence to pivot here simply for less competition. |

**No alternative clears a replacement threshold on desk evidence alone.** Stronger growth is not enough: it must offer a reachable buyer, a recurring unresolved task, manageable implementation and credible willingness to pay.

## A more precise launch hypothesis

Customer: an owner-operated food/drink shop with one to three locations, several suppliers, and delivery/invoice exceptions still passed around through paper, photos and messages.

Problem: evidence and follow-up are lost between receipt of goods and final resolution of the supplier charge.

Proposed offer: **Check a delivery, record the issue, prepare the claim and keep it visible until the supplier resolves it.**

This is a candidate workflow, not a uniqueness claim. Its competitive test is whether the owner completes it more reliably with less effort than with the installed tools.

Start with one real incident rather than requiring a complete product catalogue. Track the supplier, invoice, affected lines, pack/quantity confirmation, evidence, owner, deadline, status and resolution. Allow the owner to use the supplier's existing claims channel. Do not claim that an exported message or saved draft was actually submitted or accepted.

Potential advantages to prove: faster first use; minimal duplicate entry; useful handling of different supplier formats; clear staff-to-owner handoff; dependable evidence; and a resolution trail. Multilingual help or OCR can support these, but neither is a durable advantage alone.

Measure separate outcomes: amount queried, amount accepted, credit received/applied, and estimated time saved. Do not count every detected issue as recovered money. Maintain disputed and rejected outcomes too.

## What this means for the app

1. Preserve existing shop data and stable niche identifiers. Change grouping and visibility rather than deleting workspaces.
2. Separate **product range**, **operating methods** and **sales channels** in onboarding. For example: packaged groceries + weighed produce + market trading.
3. Show a small, relevant set of tools for launch customers. Keep research concepts explicitly marked as concepts.
4. Avoid promises such as automatic tax filing, complete traceability or live stock unless the relevant capabilities and inputs actually exist. The reviewed registry still contains broad promises that need an implementation check before public use.
5. A till-independent interface still needs reliable inputs. Received stock alone does not reveal units sold. Forecasting, live stock, replenishment and unexplained-loss reports need sales data, other stock movements and reconciliation.
6. Before real pilots, complete reliable backup/sync, permissions and the handoff between staff capture and owner review. This assessment does not certify those capabilities as production-ready.
7. Keep the existing React/Vite codebase for this test. A framework migration would not resolve the market question.

## Two-week validation plan

Aim for 15 initial interviews: three convenience, three off-licence, three international grocers, three pet shops and three beauty/hair-supply stores. This is directional screening, not a representative market survey. Increase the strongest candidate to at least five independent owners before committing major niche-specific development. If access to another current niche is much better, substitute it for beauty rather than forcing the allocation.

### Days 1–4: observe before demonstrating

- Ask for the last delivery shortage, price discrepancy, unresolved credit or missed special order.
- Ask the owner to show the actual process and existing software.
- Capture frequency in the past four weeks, affected value, minutes spent, person responsible, and final outcome.
- Establish what already works well and what would make an additional app unacceptable.
- Ask how they choose and pay for software; do not treat polite interest as a buying signal.

### Days 5–8: test a short workflow

- Use a real anonymised example with permission; retain only necessary research data.
- Compare the current process with the proposed workflow on the same task.
- Observe time, errors, help required and repeated entry.
- Where possible, let the owner demonstrate the incumbent solution rather than asserting missing features from marketing pages.
- Request access to a real pilot and nominate its daily user and outcome.

### Days 9–14: make a provisional selection

- Invite the strongest three shops into a monitored pilot.
- Test a concrete fee and paid-pilot terms; do not infer price acceptance from 'sounds reasonable'.
- Distinguish historical clean-up savings from ongoing monthly value.
- Use at least two supplier cycles; some credit-resolution outcomes will take longer than two weeks. Keep measuring before declaring retention or recurring ROI.

Suggested internal gate, not an industry benchmark: among five well-qualified owners in a candidate segment, at least three show the same recurring problem with evidence, at least two commit to using the workflow on actual work, and at least one accepts a concrete paid pilot or paid continuation. Also require credible monthly value at least three times the proposed fee after allowing for the owner's extra effort. These small-sample gates justify the next experiment, not a claim of product-market fit.

Reasons to pause or change: the issue is infrequent; the current tool resolves it when configured correctly; benefits are only one-off; staff will not maintain inputs; access/integration costs overwhelm the benefit; no one owns the buying decision; or positive interviews fail to produce real usage and payment.

## Final decision

Retain the existing retail direction. Reduce the launch scope and qualify by an observed workflow gap. Start recruitment through convenience, off-licence and international grocery; use pet shops as the strongest existing comparison and beauty/hair-supply as a limited outside comparison. Keep the remaining categories for discovery, with clearer boundaries. Reallocate development only when demonstrated customer evidence is stronger than the current candidates.
