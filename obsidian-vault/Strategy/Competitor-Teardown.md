# Competitor Teardown

Snapshot of the UK POS landscape (researched July 2026). Prices/rates approximate — re-verify before quoting publicly.

## The incumbents

### Square
- Free plan; paid £29–£69/mo; in-person card **from 1.75%**; **no contract**.
- Strengths: default for indies, easy, loyalty/marketing/staff add-ons.
- Weakness vs us: payments-first, generic; **no UK statutory payroll / VAT / compliance depth**. Rate creep at volume (~£437/mo at £25k sales).

### SumUp
- **0.99% card** once takings pass ~£2,500/mo; cheap, flexible, cloud.
- Weakness: light back office; a payment tool that grew a POS, not a retail operating system.

### Zettle (PayPal POS)
- Simple payments + basic sales mgmt for small retailers.
- Weakness: shallow; not a serious back office.

### Epos Now
- Hardware **from £299**, **12-month minimum contract**, own Payments ~1.5%.
- Strengths: recognised, stock features (variants, POs, supplier records, low-stock alerts).
- Weakness vs us: **horizontal** across pubs/cafés/retail; upsell-heavy; not c-store-specialised; contract lock-in. **Closest competitor** — watch them.

### Shopify POS
- £5/mo Lite, £69/mo Retail; best for **online + in-store** brands; 0.5–2% surcharge on external processors.
- Weakness vs us: built for DTC/omnichannel commerce, **not corner-shop P&L, payroll or UK compliance**.

### Lightspeed Retail
- Cleaner reporting, multi-store stock transfers, reorder automation.
- Weakness: mid-market scope + price; overkill for a single independent c-store.

## The "AI POS" wave (the user's concern)
- Real trend: AI is *the* headline POS theme for 2026 — forecasting, inventory automation, customer insight. Examples like **SmartPOS AI** (cloud POS w/ fiscal compliance, inventory, loyalty, analytics) plus many vibe-coded startups.
- **But:** these are overwhelmingly **horizontal** — cart + payments + inventory + an AI-flavoured dashboard. The reporting says lots of AI-*enhanced* POS, **no evidence of depth in UK-specific statutory back office**.
- **Takeaway:** AI lowers the cost of building a *generic* POS → the generic POS is being commoditised. It does **not** lower the cost of *knowing UK retail law and getting payroll/VAT provably right*. Compete on the second thing.

## Symbol groups (Nisa, Premier, Londis, Spar, Bestway…)
- Offer buying power + a **managed EPOS** as a membership perk, delivery partnerships, group pricing, a BDM.
- Gap: you must **join the group**. **71% of stores are independent**; unaffiliated ones (below-benchmark margins) get none of this. **That's our beachhead** — give them symbol-grade back office without the membership.

## Market sizing (context)
- Retail POS software: **$29.69B (2025) → $51.84B (2030)**, ~10% CAGR. Big, growing, but crowded at the generic end.
- The **defensible niche is vertical depth**, not the horizontal middle everyone (incl. AI clones) is piling into.
